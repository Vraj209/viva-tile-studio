Act as a senior creative director, editorial web designer, 3D material
artist, motion designer, and Next.js engineer working as one coordinated
team.

Design and build a distinctive, production-quality landing page for
VAVI TILE STUDIO using Next.js, TypeScript, GSAP, and Three.js.

“From imagination to a space that is yours.”

One recognizable tile design must connect the opening, material
exploration, assembled wall, and project enquiry.

The website should feel like entering a thoughtfully curated material
gallery—not a building-supply catalogue, generic SaaS template, or

The commercial objective is a qualified custom-project enquiry.
Visitors must understand what VAVI creates, what they can bring to the

• Logo and brand assets.
• Tile photographs and artwork.
• Title and typography samples.

• Videos, 3D models, and technical documents.

External references for design inspiration only.

Do not infer additive manufacturing, a specific printer, substantial
raised relief, or a particular production method from marketing copy.

evidence supports it.

CHAPTER B — THE SIGNATURE TRANSFORMATION
Visitor question: “How can my idea become a surface?”

Continue the same recognizable artwork through four visual beats.

Each beat needs one concise explanation and a clear visual purpose.

BEAT 1: YOUR IDEA

Present a supplied artwork, pattern, or logo as the starting point.

Communicate that a photograph, sketch, or unfinished concept can
begin the conversation.

BEAT 2: YOUR TILE

Carry that artwork onto a believable tile face.

Rotate the tile enough to reveal its edge and surface response.

Use a clean artwork reveal—not a fictional printer simulation.

Show physical relief only where approved evidence supports it.

BEAT 3: YOUR COMPOSITION

Move from individual pieces into a coordinated tiled composition.

Map the artwork continuously across the pieces.
Do not repeat the entire artwork independently on every tile.

Use approved layouts and dimensions where available.
Otherwise identify the arrangement as illustrative.

BEAT 4: YOUR SPACE

Pull back or match-cut to the same design inside an interior.

Match the framing, light direction, artwork scale, and tile alignment
so the transition feels continuous.

Use a completed-project photograph only when verified.
Label a created room rendering “Concept visualization.”

Suggested desktop choreography:

Opening: hold a clear composition.
0–25%: introduce the idea.
25–50%: reveal the tile detail.
50–75%: assemble the composition.
75–100%: reveal the space.

These are starting points to tune, not rigid requirements.

Include calm reading holds.
Avoid one continuous blur of motion.

Keep the pinned visual sequence to roughly 2–2.5 viewport-heights
of scroll travel beyond its viewport—not an endless cinematic tunnel.

Pin the visual area without trapping navigation or keyboard focus.

Provide a visible way to skip to the gallery or enquiry.

Reverse scrolling must reverse the story predictably.

For mobile and reduced motion, communicate all four beats through a
short image-and-caption sequence.

Do not require hovering, 3D dragging, or hidden gestures to understand
the product.

6. 3D ART DIRECTION
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Make tiles feel like physical samples—not floating website cards.

Use believable thickness, restrained bevels, correct artwork
proportions, soft contact shadows, and controlled studio lighting.

Use non-metallic surface behavior unless the sample shows otherwise.

A gentle grazing light may reveal detail, but it must not exaggerate
unverified surface texture.

Avoid:
Mirror-like plastic.
Swollen edges.
Disconnected hovering shadows.
Excessive bloom.
Particles.
Fast spins.
Artwork stretched around tile edges.

A tile may move through space as a storytelling device.
Its shape and surface must still respect the product evidence.

Give the hero tile a constrained, damped pointer response only on
suitable fine-pointer devices.

Optional sample selection should update a visible HTML caption and
have keyboard-accessible controls.

Do not make essential information canvas-only.

Prefer one scoped canvas for the opening experience, reused across
its states.

Use optimized imagery for the gallery, process, and final enquiry,
rather than mounting a WebGL renderer in every section.

If a complex room transition cannot look convincing within the
performance budget, use a carefully matched static visualization.

Do not ship an unfinished low-poly room simply to make everything 3D.

7. MOTION SYSTEM
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Give GSAP ownership of timeline choreography and DOM transitions.

Use Three.js / React Three Fiber for scene objects and rendering.

Use one coordinated progress model.
Do not let multiple systems compete for the same transform.

Use scoped useGSAP setup and cleanup.

Make breakpoint and reduced-motion behavior explicit through
media-query variants.

Clean up interaction animations, event listeners, and delayed
callbacks—not only initial timelines.

Animate references and scene properties without React state updates
on every frame.

For on-demand rendering, request frames throughout active tweens
and damping, then stop when settled.

Pause unnecessary rendering offscreen and when the tab is hidden.

Use natural browser scrolling by default.

Do not add a smooth-scroll dependency unless it produces a tested
improvement without breaking navigation, touch behavior, or pinning.

Most interactions should use small transforms, image crops, opacity,
and underlines.

Heading reveals should be brief and restrained, with intact
accessible text.

Avoid character-by-character effects on every sentence.

Starting timing ranges:
Small interactions: approximately 180–300ms.
Editorial reveals: approximately 500–900ms.

Tune by observation, not by treating these values as universal rules.

9. ACCESSIBILITY AND PERFORMANCE
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Use visible focus states, semantic headings, clear form labels,

Make content readable at 200% zoom.

Hide decorative canvas content from assistive technology.
Expose meaningful captions and controls as HTML.

Respect prefers-reduced-motion with a genuinely simplified
experience:

No camera flights.
No perpetual rotation.
No scrubbed spatial movement.
No prolonged pinning.

Do not merely speed up the same animation.
Preserve the story through static visuals and normal document flow.

Optimize images and textures for their actual display size.

Lazy-load below-the-fold imagery.
Defer nonessential 3D code.
Reuse geometry and materials.
Use adaptive pixel density.

Do not load 8K textures simply because the visual ambition is high.

Engineering targets to measure—not claims to make before testing:

LCP at or below 2.5 seconds.
CLS at or below 0.1.
Responsive interactions around 200ms or better on the agreed profile.

Establish a deferred-scene asset budget, initially around 3MB
compressed, and adjust through documented profiling.

Reduce complexity when device performance is inadequate.

A beautifully composed static experience is preferable to a
stuttering animation.

Keep navigation, CTAs, and the form usable while optional media loads.
